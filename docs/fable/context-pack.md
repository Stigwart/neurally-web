## 4. Approach

- **Three questions:** Where is the advantage? · What must change? · How will we realise it?
- **Four stages, in this order (exact names):** **Decide → Design → Deliver → Embed.**
  Stage colours are currently blue / yellow / sage / coral. You may restyle them, but keep
  a consistent one-to-one stage identity if it still serves the redesign.
  - **Decide:** choose the few opportunities worth funding, with a value case in numbers and
    the measures each will be judged by. *(Workshop, Diagnostic, Deep Dive)*
  - **Design:** rework products, workflows, roles and decision rights, and agree which calls
    stay with people. *(Operating Model & Transformation Design)*
  - **Deliver:** test the riskiest assumptions in small, time-boxed pilots with agreed
    success measures (proceed / adapt / stop), then build and scale what works with client
    teams and partners. *(Implementation Support, Transformation Leadership)*
  - **Embed:** a named owner for each result, training on the new work, tracking against agreed
    measures until the team runs it without Neurally. *(AI Training)*
  - Advisory runs alongside any stage. **Clients can stop after any stage and keep every
    output.**
- **Home key messages (problem → message → line):**
  - AI activity, no payback → **Pick the right bets** → We find the few changes that will
    move revenue, cost or service, and put a value case behind each.
  - New tools, same old habits → **Change the work** → We redesign products, workflows and
    roles so the gains land, and say clearly what stays with people.
  - No one owns the outcome → **Get it built and live** → We lead the change with your
    teams and support the build hands-on, with partners where needed.
  - Teams unsure how to use it → **A team that runs it** → We train your people on their
    real work, not generic demos, and agree how results are measured.
- **Sizing an opportunity:** expected outcome, current performance where evidence exists,
  assumptions; costs, feasibility, readiness, dependencies, adoption, quality and risk.
  **Revenue, cost savings, released capacity and customer outcomes are kept separate.
  "Time saved creates capacity. It only becomes a cash saving when spending is reduced or
  avoided."** Evidence gaps and confidence are recorded against every estimate, so leadership
  can tell a hypothesis from a validated investment case. This is a strong differentiator,
  so use it.

## 5. Governance (strongest under-sold differentiator)

Governance is a thread through every engagement, not a separate product:
- **Human & AI accountability:** every workflow redesign sets out who is accountable, what
  stays with a person, what's automated, and how a decision can be reviewed and reversed.
- **FCA Consumer Duty & conduct risk:** for FS/insurance, designs are tested against
  Consumer Duty outcomes and conduct risk before they reach a board.
- **UK GDPR / ICO:** data use, retention and third-party processing are covered in scoping.
- **EU AI Act:** risk-category exposure is flagged early, while the design can still change.
- **Board oversight:** assumptions, risk and dependencies are made as visible as the
  opportunity.
- **Governance in the plan:** mobilisation plans include an AI governance and risk view,
  with owners and decision gates.
- Grounding (pre-Neurally): Stuart launched Compare the Market's first consumer AI
  assistant and the first ChatGPT app for insurance in an FCA, Consumer Duty environment.

## 6. Principles and boundaries

**Must do:** Business value before technology · Redesign before automation ("Automating a
poor process preserves the wrong work") · Deliberate allocation of work (what value is the
work meant to create; what should stop first; what stays human, is augmented, is
automated, or is delegated within defined controls; what roles, skills, systems, measures and
governance change around it) · Evidence before scale · Capability transfer (outputs and methods
belong to the client).

**Won't do:** sell software or resell tools (client teams or partners Neurally helps
choose and brief do the build) · generic off-the-shelf training · recruitment or extra
staffing · open-ended functional cover (fractional/interim work is fixed term and fixed outcome) · payment
by results. An NDA is standard before any substantive information is shared.
