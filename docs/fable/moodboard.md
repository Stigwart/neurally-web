# Design reference

Stuart chose a single reference: **https://genesy.framer.website/**. It's a Framer
template for AI agencies. Don't search for other references.

Look at it once: one desktop screenshot of the full page and one at mobile width, both
at reduced scale (see the screenshot rules in the brief). Scroll it once to see the
motion. Everything below is already extracted from its source, so you don't need to
re-derive it.

## What it is (extracted from the page source)

- **Palette:** near-monochrome. Black (`#000`, `#080808`, `#0D0D0D`) and white, grey
  `#999` for secondary text, and a single electric-blue accent (`#0099FF`) for links and
  emphasis.
- **Type:** **Onest** (geometric grotesque) for display and body, and **Fragment Mono**
  for small labels and index numbers. The type is large and confident, with a big weight
  and size contrast between display and body.
- **Structure:** copy-led and editorial. Numbered section markers (`/ 001 /` … `/ 010 /`)
  and small index labels (01, 02) give a systematic, technical feel. Headings split into
  two beats, e.g. "Proof in numbers. All of them still running." Hero H1: "Automation
  that reaches production and stays there."
- **Section rhythm:** hero with two CTAs and stat chips → stat tiles → expandable
  service cards → 4-step process → work → reviews → "Without / With" comparison →
  team → pricing with a toggle and a "Most taken" badge → FAQ accordion → contact.
- **Motion:** Framer appear effects. Elements rise about 16px and fade in on scroll,
  small elements scale up from 0, cards expand and collapse, and the pricing toggle
  animates its state.
- **Tone:** direct, anti-hype, short declarative lines ("No lock-in, ever").

## Borrow

- The calm monochrome base with one disciplined accent. You may keep Neurally's stage
  colours as the only other chroma, used sparingly and with meaning.
- The mono index/label system for section numbering. It suits Neurally's
  numbered ladder (00–08) and the four stages.
- The two-beat headlines and the generous whitespace between sections.
- Expandable cards for the engagement ladder and the services, a "Without / With" comparison
  (Neurally has natural material for it in §3 and §6 of the context pack), and an FAQ accordion.
- Pricing presented confidently as a feature. Neurally publishes fees and High Intent Labs
  doesn't.

## Don't borrow

- Testimonials, logo strips, case-study counts and stat tiles built on client results.
  Neurally has none of these. Use career evidence (labelled) and the published-fee and
  scope facts as the proof instead.
- The template feel. This is a reference for quality and rhythm only. Don't copy the
  layout one-to-one, and don't use its copy or assets.
- The subscription tiers and monthly/yearly toggle. These don't fit Neurally's offer.

## Push further than the reference

The brief asks for **expressive** motion, which goes beyond Genesy's simple appear
effects. Aim for one signature hero moment (a visual expression of what stays with people
and what AI takes on), scroll-linked storytelling through the four stages, and page
transitions. Keep the restraint of Genesy's palette and type so the motion feels premium
rather than busy.
